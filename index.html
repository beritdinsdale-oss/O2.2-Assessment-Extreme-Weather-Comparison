STOP 3 · THE BIG CLIMATE PICTURE

Watch the processes behind the extremes

These short scientific animations show physical processes rather than simply labeling an event as “climate change.” Use the tabs to move between them.

STRONG EVIDENCE

A small shift changes the extremes

Watch the temperature distribution move. The important change is at the hot end of the curve: temperatures that were once rare occupy more of the distribution.

Explore the science ↗ Animation: Climate Central, presented by NOAA Climate.gov.
STRONG EVIDENCE

More moisture can feed heavier rain

NOAA’s animation shows water vapor moving in an atmospheric river. A warmer atmosphere can hold more water vapor, increasing the amount available when conditions produce precipitation.

Open NOAA animation ↗ Animation: NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory.
STRONG EVIDENCE

Dryness develops through a water balance

Watch conditions progress from normal toward drought. Heat can increase evaporative demand, so precipitation is only one part of the story.

Open USGS animation ↗ Animation: U.S. Geological Survey. Public domain.
UNCERTAIN FUTURE TREND

Freezing rain needs a narrow temperature setup

Snow melts in an above-freezing layer aloft, then liquid drops pass through shallow subfreezing air near the surface and freeze on contact. Because several temperature layers must align, changes in future ice-storm frequency are harder to project than changes in extreme heat.

Open NOAA freezing-rain animation ↗ Animation: NOAA NESDIS / NASA Space Place.
