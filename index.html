STOP 3 · THE BIG CLIMATE PICTURE

What changes the odds?

Watch each process, then compare how confidently scientists can project future change.

Scientific certaintyHIGH

A warmer starting point changes the odds

The temperature distribution shifts toward warmer conditions. That makes very hot temperatures that were once rare occur more often.

Explore the science ↗ Animation: Climate Central, presented by NOAA Climate.gov.
Scientific certaintyHIGH

Moisture moves—and warmer air can carry more of it

The NASA animation shows water vapor being transported in atmospheric rivers and the precipitation that follows. A warmer atmosphere can hold more water vapor, increasing the potential for heavier precipitation when storms develop.

Explore the climate connection ↗ Animation: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio. Climate connection: U.S. EPA.
Scientific certaintyHIGH for increasing summer drought risk

Water leaves both soil and plants

Evaporation from soil and transpiration from leaves combine as evapotranspiration. Warming increases atmospheric demand for moisture, which can intensify dry conditions even when precipitation is only part of the story.

Explore Oregon drought projections ↗ Animation: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Conceptual Image Lab.
Scientific certaintyMORE UNCERTAIN / LOCATION DEPENDENT

Several temperature layers have to line up

Freezing rain requires melted precipitation aloft and a shallow layer of subfreezing air near the surface. Northern Willamette Basin modeling projects fewer freezing-rain occurrences at some lower elevations and more at some higher elevations, while some future events may accumulate more ice.

Explore the Willamette Basin research ↗ Animation: NOAA NESDIS / NASA Space Place. Future projections: Rupp et al.
Compare the certainty: Warming makes increasing heat risk relatively straightforward to project. Drought, precipitation, and especially freezing rain depend on additional processes, timing, and geography.
